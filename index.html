01The question

How can physical agents
keep getting better?

ONE SHARED AGENDA
Four connected themes.
Three robotics cases.

Reliable action in the physical world requires more than strong performance on a single benchmark. Robot policies must work with incomplete observations, changing environments, contact dynamics, and real-time safety constraints.

Physical Intelligence at Scale brings together robotics, learning, simulation, and systems researchers to examine how models, memory, and infrastructure can support cumulative improvement through experience.

We connect these discussions to concrete robot tasks, measurable failure modes, and explicit data and compute budgets.

Our aim: a shared map of deployment bottlenecks, evaluation gaps, and research priorities, developed through talks, posters, debate, and collaborative discussion.
02Research themes

Four parts of one conversation.

From what a robot learns to how that learning is retained, tested, scaled, and validated.

A

Evolving modelsVLA · WORLD MODELS · INTERACTION

How should action and predictive models improve through interaction, and how can we tell when their predictions are unreliable?

B

Agentic memoryRETAIN · RETRIEVE · REUSE

Which episodic, semantic, and procedural experience should an agent retain, update, or forget as its environment changes?

C

Simulation & renderingPHYSICS · SENSORS · FIDELITY

Which physical and sensor effects need fidelity, and where does higher throughput provide the greater benefit?

D

Training & transferCOMPUTE · OPTIMIZATION · SIM-TO-REAL

How can scalable training be designed around adaptation budgets and measurable validation on physical robots?

03Common ground

Start with the robot task.

Three proposed discussion cases connect every theme to a physical challenge. These are discussion scenarios; demonstrations have not been confirmed.

AHousehold manipulation BBimanual assembly CAdaptive locomotion

Long-horizon household manipulation

A multi-room request, moved objects, and interruptions. How can a robot use past experience without relying on stale information?

Candidate evidence

Task completion · Intervention rate
Recovery success · Incorrect memory retrieval

Models

Ground instructions, predict consequences, and revise beliefs.

Memory

Distinguish prior attempts, object knowledge, and reusable skills.

Simulation

Vary clutter, lighting, articulated objects, and human proximity.

Training

Evaluate transfer across homes under limited adaptation budgets.

Contact-rich bimanual assembly

Two manipulators perform insertion or handover under changing tolerances and friction. When is a learned recovery safe to transfer?

Candidate evidence

Held-out-tolerance success · Peak force
Retries · Failure-detection latency

Models

Represent contact transitions and flag unreliable predictions.

Memory

Link successful and failed attempts to parts and guarded procedures.

Simulation

Examine compliance, friction, force sensing, and actuator delay.

Training

Compare parameter sweeps, calibration, and transfer across setups.

Locomotion under changing conditions

A robot encounters new terrain, traction, or payload. Which experience should it reuse, and when should it revise its assumptions?

Candidate evidence

Completion · Falls and near-falls
Recovery time · Energy use

Models

Infer dynamics and predict consequences under changing loads.

Memory

Retain terrain encounters and recovery strategies without stale assumptions.

Simulation

Test terrain, contact, actuator, and sensor mismatch.

Training

Connect curriculum coverage with staged physical validation.

Call for contributions

The planned call welcomes work that connects model learning, memory, simulation, or systems to physical robot learning and evaluation.

  • Research & position papers Planned: 2–4 pages
  • Demo & benchmark reports Planned: 2 pages
  • Negative results, datasets & systems artifacts

References are excluded from the planned page limits. Contributions are intended to be non-archival. Final instructions, review criteria, and a submission link will accompany the published call.
